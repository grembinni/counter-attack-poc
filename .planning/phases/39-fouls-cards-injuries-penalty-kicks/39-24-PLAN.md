<objective>
Close UAT gap 7: add the tackle-from-behind rule — a tackle that arrives on either of the two hexes directly behind the ball carrier fouls on a defender die of 1 or 2, and the Action Log says so.

Purpose: this rule does not exist anywhere in the codebase. `FOUL_TRIGGER_DIE` is a single flat constant (1) with no directional variant, and nothing computes "behind the attacker" for any purpose.

Output: two shared geometry/threshold helpers, a threshold-aware `resolveFoulChain`, the computation wired into the tackle branch only, and a distinct log line.

## Geometry specification (resolved — do not re-derive)

The board is an ODD-Q flat-top offset grid where `q` is the column and every hex has exactly six neighbours: two at `Δq = +1`, two at `Δq = -1`, and two at `Δq = 0` (directly above and below). Home attacks `q = 36`, away attacks `q = 0` (the repo-wide `goalQ = attackingTeam === 'home' ? 36 : 0` convention).

"The 2 spaces behind the attacker" are therefore exactly the two neighbours of the carrier's hex on the side away from the goal the carrier is attacking:

- carrier on the **home** team → the two neighbours with `q === carrier.q - 1`
- carrier on the **away** team → the two neighbours with `q === carrier.q + 1`

The two `Δq = 0` neighbours are lateral, not behind, and the two `Δq = +1` (home) / `Δq = -1` (away) neighbours are in front. This yields exactly two hexes, matching the user's wording, with no ambiguity and no tie-break needed. `hexNeighbors` already returns the parity-correct neighbour set, so no ODD-Q offset table may be hand-rolled here (Phase 17.1-08 fixed exactly that bug class once already).
</objective>
