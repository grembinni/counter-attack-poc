<objective>
Wire the D-15 keeper auto-snap and the FINAL_THIRD_RESPONSE zone-crossing trigger into
`broadcastState`, resolving the sequencing hazard research flagged (Pitfall 1: a naive
placement AFTER `applyFreeMoveZoneCheck` would never fire because `ballZone` is already
overwritten) and the unspecified interaction between the NEW FINAL_THIRD_RESPONSE trigger
and the PRE-EXISTING MOVE-06 `FREE_MOVE_ATTACK`/`FREE_MOVE_DEFENSE` trigger, both of which key
off "ball enters a final third."

<ambiguity_resolution>
CONTEXT.md/RESEARCH.md do not specify what happens when a single ball movement crosses
into a final third that ALSO has zero pieces in it (so FINAL_THIRD_RESPONSE has nobody to
move) while the OPPOSITE third has pieces (so the pre-existing FREE_MOVE mechanic would
normally fire) — research's Assumption A4 flags this as unresolved. This plan's resolution,
made explicit here rather than guessed silently: **FINAL_THIRD_RESPONSE takes priority over
the legacy FREE_MOVE mechanic on any single zone-crossing.** If the entered third has at
least one eligible piece, FINAL_THIRD_RESPONSE fires and `ballZone` updates, which makes
`applyFreeMoveZoneCheck` (unchanged, running immediately after) see no crossing and stay
dormant for that action. If the entered third has zero eligible pieces, this function leaves
`ballZone` untouched so `applyFreeMoveZoneCheck` still detects the crossing and runs its
existing, unmodified opposite-third logic. The keeper D-15 snap is unconditional on either
outcome — it always fires on a fresh final-third crossing, gated only by the once-per-
possession flag. This keeps both mechanics fully functional without inventing a multi-hop
resume chain; the tradeoff (documented) is that a ball landing directly in a final third
that already has pieces in it will never ALSO trigger the opposite-third free move for that
specific crossing — a second, later crossing is required for that. Flag this design choice
to the user if it does not match their real intent.
</ambiguity_resolution>

Purpose: this is the single highest-risk piece of new FSM sequencing in the phase — get the
zone-check ordering right once, in one well-tested function, rather than scattered across
call sites.
Output: `applyResponsePhaseEntryChecks`, wired into `broadcastState` ahead of
`applyFreeMoveZoneCheck`.
</objective>
